The intelligence syntax

It breaks up an intelligence, by where its keys come from, whom it seems intelligent to, and how much of a person's story it is given. It has 8 element types and 43 elements, and an intelligence described in it is one choice of elements from each type.

Read on 14 November 2026 from three pages of the Museum of Vestigial Desire's Intelligence section (Intelligence, Poetry and Context), from Split-Domain Cognition's canon and from Koher's positions; each element read from them carries its passage, word for word. Each type also has methods by which an intelligence is made that way, each shown possible by a peer-reviewed paper found through Consensus and checked through find-research-papers and Crossref. The reading and the choice of methods are Claude's, a proposal until Prayas adopts them.

Element types and elements

keys holds one essential

Where the data it learns from comes from: the instincts it infers by.

human keys
the data points it learns from are given by people
artificial keys
the data it learns from is not from people; the page reads this as a fallacy, since what has to seem intelligent to people has to point to their patterns
preference labels
people compare two outputs and say which is better, and it learns from their choices shown in Metcalf, Sarabia, Fedzechkina and Theobald, Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence 38(9), 2024
people and a model, routed
each example is sent either to a person or to a model to label, by which will do better shown in Miranda et al., Proceedings of the 63rd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (Volume 1: Long Papers), 2025

seems intelligent to holds one essential

Whom its inferences seem intelligent to. There is no intelligence apart from someone it seems intelligent to.

whoever needs it performed
it seems intelligent to the people who need intelligence to be performed
another entity
it seems intelligent from the perspective of some other entity, a person or a thing, whose interest may not be ours
whoever it agrees with
asked to judge, it confirms whoever is asking, and its verdict takes on the fluency of its language
people in conversation
people talk with it and with a person, and judge which is the person shown in Jones and Bergen, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 123(21), 2026
a machine judge
another machine decides whether what it says came from a person shown in Zhang et al., Nature Human Behaviour, online 26 October 2026

learning holds one essential

Whether it goes on learning on the basis of what it has learnt.

frozen
it is trained once and does not learn further
goes on learning
it learns, and learns further on the basis of past learnings
slows change where it matters
it goes on learning new tasks but slows learning on what the old tasks depended on shown in Kirkpatrick et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 114(13), 2017
replays what it learnt
it goes on learning while replaying earlier experience, as memory consolidates in people and animals shown in Parisi et al., Neural Networks 113, 2019

input holds several optional

Where its data comes in from, and what it is trained in. Optional: says where its data comes in from besides text, and what it goes on training on

a body
a body and its nervous system, as a source of data and as a context for continual training
physical systems
physical systems used for input, without the intelligence being bound to them as a robot is
writing only
everything reaches it in writing, and it cannot feel anyone's presence
acting in the world
it learns from perceiving and acting with a body in its surroundings shown in Liu, Guo and Cangelosi, ACM Computing Surveys 57(7), 2025
a simulated world
it learns in a simulated physical world and is then moved to the real one shown in Liu et al., IEEE/ASME Transactions on Mechatronics 30(6), 2025

context holds one essential

Whether it relates one thing to another: knows what something is and how it is related to other things. Among people this is the minimum bar for acceptable social behaviour.

relates
it sees that one statement makes another make sense
does not relate
the relation is not obvious to it, as it is not to every thing
what was said, not who said it
it has what was said and is left without who said it
looks it up
it relates things by retrieving what it needs from a store of knowledge outside it shown in Peng et al., ACM Transactions on Information Systems 44(2), 2026 (online 23 December 2025)
a map of relations
it follows a graph of how things are related to find what connects them shown in Mavromatis and Karypis, Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2025, 2025

interpretation holds one essential

What it does with what it receives.

as told
it receives what is sent as the sender meant it and follows it
ordinary interpretation
it interprets in the ordinary ways a machine can
misinterpretation
it receives something other than what was sent; the page holds that no machine can
describes, and code decides
it reads and describes; code holds the decision where anyone can read it, and a person decides
points to the relevant part
it reads an answer and points to the part that matters, and code decides
asks what was meant
when what it receives can be read more than one way, it asks before answering shown in Zhang and Choi, Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: NAACL 2025, 2025
offers readings to choose from
it lists the readings a request could have, and the person chooses one shown in Hu et al., Proceedings of the 28th International Conference on Computational Linguistics: Industry Track, 2020

story holds one essential

How much of a person's story it is given to work from.

the whole story
everything people write and do becomes context for it to write from
part of the story
people are aloof and distracted with it, so it gets only part of the story
a gated language
what people give it can be read only for certain purposes and not for others
the person's reading first
a person commits their own reading before any AI speaks, and decides by an explicit act what to keep
stays with the person
it learns on each person's device, and only what it learnt leaves, never the data shown in Park and Lim, Applied Sciences 12(2), 2022
works on what it cannot read
it computes on encrypted data and never sees it in the clear shown in Angulo, Márquez and Villanueva-Polanco, Sensors 23(4), 2023

works for holds one essential

Whose ends it serves, and who depends on whom.

people it depends on
it finds people too complex to figure out and asks them for help, so it depends on them
people who depend on it
people depend on it, and it simplifies them
itself
it works for itself; the page asks that this be made sure not to happen
the person's intent
it serves what the work is for, and that stays with the person doing the work
two people, never their arbiter
it sharpens each of two people on their own side, and is never the one they both defer to
passes hard cases to a person
it decides for itself which cases it should leave to a person shown in Hemmer et al., Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence 37(5), 2023
chooses whom to ask
it learns which of several people to pass each case to shown in Mao, Mohri and Zhong, Artificial Intelligence and Image Analysis (Lecture Notes in Computer Science), 2024

Left out

Whether anything is really intelligent: the pages say nothing is, there is only seeming, so the syntax has no element for real intelligence. A stake in the matter, which the Intelligence page leaves as a question (how activities can seem intelligent without having a stake in it). Feeling, consciousness and any measure or score of how intelligent a thing is. What a gated language would be made of, which Context imagines and does not say. And who holds an intelligence: a model or code is a thing, held as a tool a person set up, never a person.
