# Changelog

What changed in SYNTAX, newest first. The version is in `VERSION` and shown on every page; `build.py` stops if `VERSION` and the newest entry here disagree. To add an entry: raise `VERSION` (the middle number for something people can use, the last for a fix), add a section at the top with the same number and the date, and build.

## 0.16.1 — 13 November 2026

- *a small copy taught by a bigger model* is out of the NSM syntax: the bigger model may write text, and an NSM never writes text or comes from a model that does. *an adapter on a trained model* now says the model under it does not write text, and the syntax's *left out* says an NSM never starts from such a model or is taught by one.

## 0.16.0 — 13 November 2026

- The NSM syntax has eleven more elements, each shown possible by a peer-reviewed paper found through Consensus and checked through find-research-papers and Crossref: *does* counts and notices the unusual; *reads* a short video clip; *answers with* several kinds, a mask, a stretch of time and points on a body; *starts from* a small copy taught by a bigger model; *learns from* the cases it asks you to label, normal cases only, and what it meets after it is set up. No NSM built so far uses them, and ungli marks them not yet used. The NSM syntax now has 53 elements, and 70 with the code blocks.

## 0.15.2 — 13 November 2026

- kahen's record is back in ungli's readings, with its result (108 of 179, where always answering the same gets 107) and the warning drawn from it, and is still not listed among the exemplars or here. ungli shows the records of weak results as they stand.

## 0.15.1 — 13 November 2026

- The NSM syntax's page, and ungli, say that NSM here stands for Narrow Small Model and is not the Natural Semantic Metalanguage, Anna Wierzbicka's framework in linguistics.

## 0.15.0 — 13 November 2026

- The NSM syntax has three optional element types for the plain code beside an NSM: *code takes* (one NSM's answer, several NSMs' answers, the input itself, a list written in advance), *code does* (checks, looks up, counts, compares with a line, routes, filters, assembles) and *code gives* (a decision, a name or none, a flag for a person, something made from pieces written in advance, the input passed on, don't know to a person). Each element names the NSM where code already does it. ungli shows them as code blocks in a colour of their own and reads them from here.

## 0.11.0 — 13 November 2026

- A new syntax, **band**: a band of individual practitioners, each doing their own thing, who show and publish together in episodes. Its element types are the curator, the people, the question, the way in, the space, the signing and the episode, read from Bairang's own documents. Bairang is the band described in it. It is no longer a page of its own in the menu, and /bairang/ redirects to /band/.
- The narrow small model syntax is now named **NSM** (his word), at /nsm/; /narrow-small-model/ and /small-model/ redirect there. Its element types now read as one sentence about an NSM: it does, reads, answers with, starts from, learns from and runs on. ungli, its builder, has its own interface: a task laid out as a line of steps, blocks dragged into an NSM's six slots, and a finger over the step being read.

## 0.10.0 — 13 November 2026

- The small model syntax is now the **narrow small model** syntax: narrow is the size of a model's answer, a kind, a span, an item, a number or codes, never a sentence, and not the size of the model. Its builder is now **ungli**, at [ungli.syntax.prayasabhinav.net](https://ungli.syntax.prayasabhinav.net), named for *ungli rakhna*, to put a finger on the exact spot, which is all a model there does. ungli has its own mark: one fingertip resting on one cell of a row.
- Every old address redirects: /small-model/ to /narrow-small-model/, /small-model.json to /narrow-small-model.json, and the builder's addresses, here and at small-model.syntax.prayasabhinav.net, to ungli.

## 0.9.0 — 13 November 2026

## 0.8.0 — 13 November 2026

- A sixth syntax, **small model**: a model that does one job and answers with a kind, a span, an item, a number or codes, never with sentences, and leaves the decision to code. Its element types are the job, what it reads, what it answers, the base it starts from, where its labels come from and where it runs. It leaves out any model that writes.
- [The small model builder](https://syntax.prayasabhinav.net/small-model/builder/): choose one element of any type and it shows the nearest of the 18 small models I have built or designed so far, with what each was measured to do and on which test. No predicted figures. Each model has its own address there, and the syntax's page lists them all.

## 0.7.0 — 19 October 2026

- Every element type of every syntax now says whether it is **essential** or **optional**, so completeness can be checked by type: a thing described in a syntax is complete when every essential type is decided, and an optional type left open does not make it incomplete. Each syntax's page marks its types, and an optional type says what deciding it does. The marks are written at each syntax's source: boardgameBuilder's `palette.py`, Zariia's `record-syntax.json`, Split-Domain Cognition's `personhood-syntax.json` and `life-syntax.json`, and the Museum of Vestigial Desire's `framework-syntax.json`. Three types are optional so far: theme and simulation in the board game, and rooms in the framework. The build refuses a syntax whose element types are not marked.

## 0.5.1 — 17 October 2026

- The personhood syntax says in so many words that things are excluded and animals are partially excluded: an animal holds intent, and is excluded from language, judgement and residue.

## 0.5.0 — 17 October 2026

- The fourth syntax, **life**: an animal's life, broken into place, food, action, body and signal, each either the animal's own or taken by use. An animal holds every part of it. It is read from Split-Domain Cognition's `life-syntax.json`, and its instance is the animal-rights variant.
- Personhood is partial for an animal: in the personhood syntax an animal holds intent and no other part.
- Every syntax's page has a *Left out* section: what the syntax does not describe, or who cannot hold a part of it, said in words and read from the syntax's own source, so an exclusion is never only an absence from a list. The build refuses a syntax that does not state it.
- A holder that is not a person, a model or code, is marked as a *thing*: it holds a part only as a tool a person set up, and has no personhood. *Left out* and *thing* join the words on the home page.

## 0.4.0 — 17 October 2026

- The third syntax, **personhood**: a piece of work in which language work and judgement work are both in play, broken into intent, language, judgement and residue, each held by one of the person, other people, a model under negotiation, or code. It is read from Split-Domain Cognition's `personhood-syntax.json`. Its instances are Split-Domain Cognition, and Koher as its child.
